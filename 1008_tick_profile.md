## What this is not

- Eight residents on the Stage 1 maps only; it says nothing yet about 100 residents, chunk streaming, or models.

## Notes

**Headline.** The legacy engine on the 8-resident village takes a mean 16.2 ms per tick (p50 15.2, p99 27.1, max 58.0) against a 50 ms budget. 4 of 108000 ticks exceeded 50 ms; 2097 (1.9%) exceeded 25 ms. The tick does not degrade over 90 game minutes (running p50 15.1 at 40k ticks, 15.2 at 108k).

**Where the time goes.** Two non-registry parts of `Engine.step` take 59.7% of the mean tick: `_sync_slot_ids_and_events` (5.6 ms, 34.5%) and `human_agent.process_escalations` (4.1 ms, 25.2%). Next is `world_agent.process_queues` (1.5 ms, 9.0%) and the `roster_driver` step hook (0.94 ms, 5.8%). All 33 registry entries together (the `instinct_*`/`tool_*` systems) cost about 2.15 ms in total (about 13%); the heaviest, `instinct_move`, is 0.33 ms. Replacing or rewriting the instincts will not move the tick budget; the per-tick bookkeeping around them will. Drill-down inside the two big parts (cProfile) has not been done.

**`world_contract` cost.** Rebuilding the wall list and sha256 every tick costs 0.43 ms per direct call (p99 0.77, max 8.5), plus `wall_boxes` and the `json.dumps` feeding the hash called again from `roster_driver` and `motion_limits` (about 0.11 + 0.05 + 0.11 + 0.03 + 0.05 + 0.02 ms). The sum of these probes is under 1 ms (about 5% of the tick) at 8 residents on 4 maps. It scales with wall count, not resident count, so it matters for chunked worlds. `spatial_slide` is 0.18 ms per tick over 8 calls.

**Unattributed residual.** 0.84 ms (5.2%): the slide reconcile loop, copies and glue between timed parts.

**Timer overhead.** Not instrumented, 3600 ticks (same seed): mean 15.55, p50 14.71, p95 21.65, p99 25.39 ms. Instrumented 108000 ticks: mean 16.23, p50 15.20. The difference is about 0.5 to 0.7 ms per tick (3 to 5%), comparing runs of different length, so treat it as approximate. Ratios between parts are the useful figure, not the absolute values.

**Caveats.**
- The early part of the run may have been perturbed by other CPU load on the machine (the lock was taken, but other sessions' background work was not controlled). The running p50 was flat across the run, so the effect looks small. Not verified.
- Resident memory (RSS) was not recorded by this tool; there is no memory figure here.
- 8 residents only. The numbers say nothing about 100 residents, chunk streaming, or model calls. Per-resident costs (`process_escalations`, `_sync_slot_ids_and_events`) are the ones to re-measure at scale.
- The full-run JSON is `.runtime/tick-profile-full.json` (gitignored, not committed). Regenerate with `tools/profile_tick.py --md 1008_tick_profile.md`.

## Drill-down: what is inside the two big parts

cProfile of 600 ticks (after 100 warm-up ticks), same seed and world. cProfile slows the run: `Engine.step` costs 29.6 ms/tick under it against 15.5 ms normally, and it inflates call-heavy code most, so read these as a ranking and as call counts, not as milliseconds. Cumulative times of nested callers overlap and `flush_memory_graph_queue` is recursive (its apparent 28.6 ms is double counting; its real cost is about 0.1 ms). Script was not committed; rerun with `cProfile` around 600 `engine.step()` calls after `harness.headless.boot_engine(1008)`.

**The same function is called over and over per resident per tick.** `ResidentStore.ensure_resident` runs 24 times per tick (8 residents x 3 callers) and costs 10.5 ms/tick under cProfile, the biggest single item. The callers are `human_runtime.process_escalations` (3.5), `world_runtime._queue_due_reasoning_reviews` (3.5) and `ResidentStore.sync_live_population` (3.5, called from `_sync_slot_ids_and_events`). 86% of it is `_ensure_behavior_state` (9.05), which re-runs personality (3.3), pressures (1.65), tasks (1.0), crafting (0.8) and goals (0.6) "ensure" passes on residents that are already initialised. The work is idempotent, so the repeated calls change nothing.

**Deep copies dominate that cost.** `copy.deepcopy` is called about 2.66 million times in 600 ticks (4,400 per tick), the top self-time item at 3.1 ms/tick. Callers: `ensure_task_state` and `ensure_crafting_state` (about 720 and 680 calls per resident-tick), `sync_baseline_provisioning` (1.3 ms/tick) and `map_contract.copy_boxes` (3.0 ms/tick, 5,888 calls; this is the `world_contract` wall-list copy).

**Other items in `_sync_slot_ids_and_events`:** `_sync_occupation_provisioning` (7.1 ms/tick under cProfile; per-resident `_sync_resident_construction` 2.8, `review_resident_baseline_provisioning` 1.8, `_sync_managed_resident_tasks` 1.8). `_sync_inventory_defaults` is 0.1.

**Other items in `process_escalations`:** `ops.build_candidate` 2.4 ms/tick (4,800 calls) and `routing.select_processing_batch` 0.4.

**Outside the two parts:** `map_geo.entities_in_radius` 2.3 ms/tick, all from `perception.queries.nearby_entity_ids` (16 calls per tick), and `_nearest_entity` 1.9.

**Reading for the migration.** The cost is not instinct maths: it is idempotent per-resident "ensure/sync" passes over nested Python dicts that run every tick whether anything changed or not. A dirty flag or revision per resident record (skip when nothing changed) would remove most of it without changing behaviour. This has NOT been tried; it is a measurement, not a change, and the legacy code was not edited.

## Round 2 changes and re-measurement (R2-H1)

Changes since the profile above (all behaviour-preserving; strict golden compare on exact state hashes identical for 1 and 2, and regenerated with an explained diff for the clamp fix): exact `cdist` helper at 11 call sites (9cc6ac6), 30 dead `clamp_` statements fixed (572d51f; golden regenerated, resident fields identical), `gc.freeze()` policy with thaw on reset (c5a603c), exact per-tick resident glue optimisations (5f3d5d4: one `tolist()` for the snapshot, memoised `normalize_traits`, no `deepcopy` of defaults that already exist).

**Controlled A/B (MEASURE lock, 4000 ticks, seed 1008, background writes ON as in the server, alternating runs of the tree before 5f3d5d4 and the current tree; the first pair was discarded because another session ran CPU work at 04:11 to 04:12).** Two clean pairs, before / after:

**Full instrumented profile after all Round 2 changes (36000 ticks, MEASURE lock; the earlier profile above ran 108000 ticks, so the tails are not strictly comparable):** mean 14.72, p50 13.69, p95 21.36, p99 24.68, max 44.56 ms; 329 of 36000 ticks above 25 ms (0.9%), none above 50 ms. **Spike on tick%40==1 steps, background writes OFF vs ON (4000 ticks):** OFF mean 30.6 ms (p50 30.2), ON mean 16.4 to 17.2. What remains with writes ON (cProfile of 50 such steps, a ranking, not milliseconds): `_sync_slot_ids_and_events` 31% (of which `_sync_occupation_provisioning` 0.37 s of 1.67 s, `ensure_resident` 0.39 s), `copy.deepcopy` 0.31 s (195k calls), `process_escalations` 17%, roster driver hook 13%, `world_contract`/`copy_boxes` (wall-box deepcopy) 13%. No file writes appear. The remaining spike is resident provisioning/construction sync plus deep copies of state and wall boxes.

**GC (model-plane-free run):** with the freeze policy a full collection of the loaded world takes 0.0 ms against 41 to 48 ms without it; over 4000 ticks the engine alone triggered 2 to 3 generation-0 collections and no generation-1 or -2 collections either way, so the 57 ms pause seen in the Gate A models-on run comes from the model-plane allocations (not measured here).
